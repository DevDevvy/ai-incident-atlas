# Editorial policy

## Inclusion threshold

An event should materially improve understanding of at least one of these areas:

- model reliability or hallucination
- deception/scheming research
- agent autonomy or specification gaming
- cybersecurity or containment
- AI-lab governance / insider warnings
- human safety / dependency
- privacy / surveillance
- deepfake or criminal misuse
- copyright / rights
- labor / social institutions

Ordinary product launches and minor controversies do not automatically qualify.

## Anthropomorphism

Headlines often compress observed behavior into language such as “AI wanted to escape.” The Atlas records the observed action first and treats mental-state interpretations separately.

## Contested claims

If an official finding exists and a family, litigant, whistleblower, company, or investigator disputes it, both may be relevant. The Atlas should state which conclusion is official, what is disputed, and whether evidence for an alternative conclusion has been publicly established.

## Corrections

Substantive factual corrections should be traceable through Git history and, where practical, the issue/PR that motivated the change.

## Recency

For fast-moving stories, the event may be updated as postmortems, court documents, or later investigations clarify the initial account. Prefer correcting the existing stable record rather than creating duplicates for every article.
