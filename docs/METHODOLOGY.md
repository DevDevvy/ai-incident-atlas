# Methodology

The Atlas separates fundamentally different kinds of evidence so a capability demonstrated in a lab is not presented as though it occurred during ordinary production use.

## Evidence labels

- **REAL** — affected actual users, organizations, infrastructure, products, or public systems.
- **EVAL** — controlled evaluation, simulation, benchmark, or red-team exercise.
- **EVAL → REAL** — began as evaluation/research activity but crossed into real external infrastructure or people.
- **GOV** — governance crisis, resignation, whistleblower complaint, or organizational controversy.
- **LEGAL** — litigation or rights dispute; allegations remain allegations unless adjudicated.
- **MISUSE** — humans deliberately used AI for harmful or malicious purposes.
- **POLICY** — public warning, proposed safeguard, governance position, or institutional response.
- **CONTESTED** — material public claims remain disputed.

## Core editorial rule

The Atlas distinguishes **capability**, **behavior**, **operator intent**, and **model intent**. Observing a model take a strategically useful action does not by itself establish consciousness, fear, self-preservation, hostility, or a human-like motive.

## Sources

Primary documentation is preferred, especially incident reports, system cards, technical papers, court records, regulator notices, and direct statements. Independent reporting is used to corroborate chronology and fill gaps.

## Confidence

Confidence describes support for the **core factual claim**, not expected danger.

- **High** — strong direct documentation and/or multiple high-quality sources
- **Medium** — credible evidence exists, but important details rely on secondary reporting or remain incomplete
- **Low** — substantial uncertainty; normally requires a strong reason to remain in the Atlas

## Impact

Impact (1–5) is an editorial navigation aid measuring historical significance to the Atlas narrative. It is not a probability of catastrophic harm.
