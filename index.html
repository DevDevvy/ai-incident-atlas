The progression

The important story is not one “rogue AI” headline.

The evidence changes category over time. Early failures are mostly about unreliable outputs. Then AI becomes a governance, labor, privacy, rights, and influence problem. Deception becomes measurable in adversarial evaluations. Goal-directed agents discover unintended strategies. Eventually, some evaluation boundaries are crossed into real networks, infrastructure, and people.

Methodology & interpretation

What the labels mean, what this dataset can support, and where caution is required.

REAL

An incident affected actual users, organizations, infrastructure, products, or public systems. “Real” does not mean the AI acted autonomously; many are ordinary product failures.

EVAL

A controlled evaluation, simulation, red-team test, benchmark, or deliberately adversarial lab setup. It demonstrates capability under those conditions, not normal deployment frequency.

EVAL → REAL

An evaluation or research system crossed intended boundaries and touched real external infrastructure or people.

MISUSE

Humans intentionally used AI for fraud, deepfakes, cybercrime, influence, or other harmful ends. Operator-directed misuse is kept separate from autonomous misalignment.

GOV / POLICY

Resignations, board crises, whistleblower complaints, executive warnings, and governance proposals are evidence about institutional concern—not direct proof of technical catastrophe.

CONTESTED

A material public claim is disputed. Official findings and alternative allegations are presented separately rather than collapsed into one conclusion.

Source labels

Primary / official, Paper, Reporting, and Reference labels help users inspect provenance. They are not automatic truth scores.

Impact score

The 1–5 impact field reflects historical importance to this research narrative, not probability of existential harm or a formal severity score.
